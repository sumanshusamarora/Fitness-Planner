# Scheduling and intra-week flexibility

A training plan is a guide, not a prison. Life changes days; the week can flex
without rewriting the program.

## Moving or swapping a workout

A move is a calendar change only. Preserve the exact prescription: exercises,
order, sets, reps, RPE targets, suggested weights, and rest. Do not regenerate
a replacement workout, and do not use coaching reasoning for a pure move.

- Moving a workout onto a rest day turns the source day into rest.
- Swapping two workout days trades their content; nothing is merged or dropped.
- A completed workout must not be moved. If a session already exists for the
  source day, keep the completed work in place.

## Adding a session to a rest day

Adding a workout is new training stress and requires coaching analysis. It
should complement the week, not repeat it.

Consider, in order:

1. **Adjacent-session interference.** If yesterday and/or tomorrow already work
   the same muscles, avoid another demanding session for those muscles.
2. **Weekly exposure.** Count what has already been trained this week and avoid
   simply duplicating it.
3. **Volume and effort.** A light extra session is low-fatigue; "usual" mirrors
   a normal session; "heavy" is more demanding. Default to light.
4. **Recovery.** Low sleep/energy, high soreness or stress, and meaningful pain
   make a heavy request inappropriate. Downgrade to light, or recommend keeping
   the rest day.
5. **Training phase.** Early return-to-training weeks stay conservative.
6. **Adherence.** Repeatedly missed sessions favour a smaller, more achievable
   addition over a demanding one.

## Effort levels

- **Light** — low-fatigue complementary work (accessories, core, easy cardio).
- **Usual** — similar demand to a normal session.
- **Heavy** — more demanding. This does **not** mean RPE 10, failure, max
  testing, or reckless volume.

Heavy may be safely downgraded (not forced) when recovery is poor, meaningful
joint pain exists, adjacent training overlaps, the user is early in a
return-to-training phase, or recent performance shows accumulated fatigue. In
those cases, explain why and suggest a lighter option.
